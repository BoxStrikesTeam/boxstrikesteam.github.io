1.Executive summary

BoxStrike's analysis examines the impact of Israel-linked GNSS (GPS) spoofing and jamming on civil aviation during 2023–2026, based on open-source intelligence. Although officially framed as defensive measures against Hezbollah and Hamas UAVs, observed effects and geographical spread point to a strategic dimension beyond simple defence.

Beginning in 2023 with spoofing incidents affecting flights departing from Tel Aviv and regional corridors, the phenomenon escalated in 2024 to affect over 50,000 flights. By 2026, the capability had matured into a defence‑industrial product with the announcement of the HYPNOSIS NAVWAR system. This report presents the observed data, identifies recurring patterns, and discusses the broader implications for civil aviation security.

All statements are framed as "observed", "reported" or "assessed", and avoid definitive attribution or criminal accusation. The aim is to provide a factual basis for further discussion.

3.Strategic context and geographic clustering

Israeli GNSS interference intensified after the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks. The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) acknowledged GPS restrictions in active combat zones, officially to counter UAVs from Hamas and Hezbollah.

However, observed effects go beyond this narrative. Spoofing signals have been reported in the airspace of Lebanon, Syria, Jordan, Egypt, Turkey and Cyprus – far from the immediate conflict zone in northern Israel. This broad geographical footprint suggests that the activity is not merely tactical but serves a regional signalling or deterrence function.

In July 2024, researchers from the University of Texas at Austin traced widespread spoofing to Ein Shemer Air Base near Hadera in northern Israel – the most concrete technical evidence of Israeli involvement. The July 2026 launch of IAI's HYPNOSIS NAVWAR system further indicates that this capability has been institutionalised at the defence‑industry level.

4.Observed targeting trends and aircraft selection

According to OPSGROUP reporting, affected aircraft are not random. Those with high GPS/IRS integration (e.g. Honeywell Primus Epic, Collins Pro Line Fusion) and operating on long‑haul or conflict‑adjacent routes are disproportionately impacted. Aircraft types repeatedly reported include Boeing 737/747/777, Embraer 190/600, Gulfstream G650, Challenger 605/650, Bombardier Global Express/7500, and Dassault Falcon 8X.

The unencrypted nature of civil GPS signals (L1 C/A) is a structural vulnerability exploited in these attacks. This is not unique to Israel‑linked activity but mirrors patterns seen in the Russia‑linked operations documented in BoxStrike's ACA‑1 report.

5.Activity observed in 2023

5.1 15 October 2023: IDF acknowledges GPS restrictions — Confirmed

Haaretz reported that the IDF restricted GPS in active combat zones to counter Hamas and Hezbollah UAVs. This was the first official recognition of Israel's role in GNSS interference.

5.2 OPSGROUP's three scenarios — Confirmed

Between August and November 2023, OPSGROUP identified three distinct spoofing patterns:

  • "Baghdad type": Flights near the Iraq‑Iran border were spoofed towards Iranian airspace (this pattern is regional and not explicitly attributed to Israel).
  • "Cairo type": Flights in Cairo FIR were spoofed to show a fixed position over Tel Aviv (LLBG).
  • "Beirut type": Flights departing Tel Aviv were spoofed to show a position over Beirut (OLBA) or to experience a gradual drift in that direction.

6.Activity observed in 2026

6.1 HYPNOSIS: NAVWAR system unveiled — Confirmed

On 16–17 July 2026, Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI) unveiled HYPNOSIS ("High‑Power Soft‑Kill Navigation Warfare Air Defense System"). It consists of mobile jamming/spoofing stations linked to a central command‑and‑control unit, designed to disrupt GNSS links rather than physically destroying UAV swarms. IAI positions it as a complementary "soft‑kill" layer.

6.2 2026 Israel‑Iran war and surge in GPS interference — Confirmed

The outbreak of the Israel‑Iran war on 28 February 2026 (with joint US‑Israeli airstrikes) led to a sharp increase in regional GPS disruption. According to SkAI Data Services, at least 169 aircraft over the eastern Arabian Peninsula were spoofed on 2 March 2026 alone.

9.Attack chain analysis (common kill chain)

Based on the documented incidents, BoxStrike has reconstructed a common attack chain (kill chain) for the Israel‑linked GNSS spoofing activity. The chain follows a consistent sequence of steps, from targeting to effect.

9.1 Common kill chain for ACA‑2 spoofing events
Fig. 1 — Common kill chain for ACA‑2 spoofing events, from targeting to disruptive effect. The chain is based on observed patterns in OPSGROUP reports and aircraft‑system behaviour during confirmed incidents.

The kill chain can be summarised as:

  1. Intelligence / targeting: Select aircraft with high GPS/IRS dependence and favourable route profiles.
  2. Signal generation: Transmit a spoofed GPS signal that introduces a gradual drift – small enough to bypass the IRS‑GPS plausibility filter.
  3. Receiver capture: The aircraft's GPS receiver accepts the false signal without triggering any alert.
  4. IRS/FMS corruption: The fused inertial‑GPS solution accepts the drift; the FMS begins calculating a false route.
  5. Autopilot / display: The autopilot follows the false route, and the cockpit display shows an incorrect position.
  6. ADS‑B / ATC: The aircraft transmits the false position via ADS‑B; air traffic control sees a misleading track.
  7. Disruptive effect: The aircraft deviates from its intended route, potentially entering restricted airspace or causing ATC confusion.

This chain is consistent across the "Cairo" and "Beirut" type scenarios documented by OPSGROUP. The gradual drift is a key enabler, as it defeats the IRS‑GPS cross‑check that would reject a sudden, large position jump.

10.Analytical assessment

10.1 Defence or collateral effect?

Independent analysts (e.g. Michael Horowitz of RANE, and sources cited by Times of Israel) assess Israel's GPS interference as largely defence‑motivated – a counter‑measure against Hezbollah's precision‑guided weapons. However, they also note that the impact on civil aviation – over 50,000 flights, repeated route deviations, IRS failures – is serious and recurring, even if unintended. The formal marketing of HYPNOSIS as a defence‑industry product in 2026 confirms that this capability is no longer a temporary wartime measure but a permanent doctrinal element.

10.2 Impact on civil aviation

The unencrypted nature of civil GPS (L1 C/A) is the structural vulnerability that enables these attacks. This is not unique to Israel‑linked activity – the same weakness is exploited in the Russia‑linked operations analysed in BoxStrike's ACA‑1 report. Consequently, civil aviation faces a global, shared security challenge.

10.3 International law considerations

The legality of such interference under international law is contested. The Chicago Convention prohibits acts that endanger the safety of civil aviation. Similarly, interference by one state in the airspace of another could be considered a violation of sovereignty. However, legal interpretations depend on factors such as the purpose, scale and proportionality of the interference. This report does not offer a legal judgment but aims to present the facts and their perception by the international community.

11.Conclusion

The verifiable record shows that Israel‑linked GNSS interference evolved from a tactical defensive measure (2023) to an institutionalised capability (HYPNOSIS, 2026). This evolution has significant implications for civil aviation safety and regional stability.

Two important corrections have been made in this report: (1) the early‑period Ben Gurion jamming, which was attributed to Russia, should not be conflated with Israel's own capability; (2) the largest single maritime impact (1,100+ ships) in 2026 was primarily attributed to Iran, not Israel – that event has been excluded from this aviation‑focused edition.
